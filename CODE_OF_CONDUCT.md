# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want `codex-plugin-glab` to be a useful, respectful, and welcoming open-source project for contributors with different backgrounds, identities, experience levels, and technical viewpoints.

Participation in this project should make it easier for people to collaborate, learn, review code, report problems, and improve the project safely.

## Expected behavior

Examples of constructive participation include:

- discussing technical ideas rather than attacking people;
- giving specific, actionable, and respectful feedback;
- acknowledging mistakes and correcting them;
- respecting different levels of experience and communication styles;
- protecting private information and credentials;
- keeping reviews focused on the contribution and project goals.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, discrimination, threats, intimidation, or sustained personal attacks;
- sexualized language or unwanted sexual attention;
- doxxing or publishing another person's private information without permission;
- deliberately exposing credentials, private repository data, or security-sensitive information;
- spam, deliberate disruption, or repeated bad-faith behavior;
- retaliation against someone for reporting a conduct or security concern.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, project-maintained communication channels, and situations where someone is clearly representing the project.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, hide, reject, or remove content that violates these expectations. Repeated or severe violations may result in temporary or permanent restrictions on participation.

Enforcement decisions should consider context, severity, impact, prior behavior, and the safety of affected participants.

## Reporting

If a conduct problem involves private information, threats, harassment, or another sensitive matter, do not post details publicly. Use a private maintainer contact channel when possible and include only the information needed to investigate the report.

Security vulnerabilities and exposed credentials should follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) rather than the conduct-reporting path.

## Good-faith participation

Disagreement about architecture, implementation, scope, or project priorities is normal. Strong technical disagreement is welcome when it remains respectful, evidence-based, and focused on the work.
